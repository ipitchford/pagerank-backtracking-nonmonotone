**Unrefereed research candidate.** No journal and no named human specialist has reviewed this manuscript. It was checked by internal AI-assisted review: a multi-perspective first round, AI-assisted adversarial audits by a different model of the first draft and of the revised version, and a verification re-review of the revised version (see [REVIEW_SUMMARY.md](REVIEW_SUMMARY.md)). That is not peer review, it is not independent reproduction, and it is not formal verification.

Object: backtracking-weighted PageRank on finite connected simple graphs. The walk lives on directed edges, the immediate return has weight μ ≥ 0 and every other continuation has weight one, and restart is spread evenly over each vertex's outgoing edges. The vertex score π_μ(i) is the tail projection. The source of the question is Glover, Jones, Kempton and Oveson, *Effects of backtracking on PageRank*, Appl. Math. Comput. 531 (2026) 130182, doi:[10.1016/j.amc.2026.130182](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amc.2026.130182). The open version used here is arXiv:[2211.13353v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13353v2), Conjectures 3.1 and 3.2.

What the package claims (section numbers refer to [paper/paper.pdf](paper/paper.pdf)):

* **Proved in writing.** Every proof is in the manuscript. Some steps are exact polynomial identities, and the accompanying code checks those.
  * Theorems 3.1 and 4.1, Corollary 4.2: the roof vertex of the five-vertex house graph has an exact cubic response. It is monotone iff ε ≤ ε_c ≈ 0.2406813. Otherwise it has exactly one strict minimum, which lies at μ = 1 iff ε = 4/9. Failure occurs even with μ restricted to [0, 1]. This refutes the coordinatewise clause of Conjecture 3.1, and hence the conjecture.
  * Proposition 4.3: the endpoint shortcut (the side of π_1 on which π_∞ lies predicts the side of π_0) is wrong for the roof iff ε > ε_0 ≈ 0.3291038.
  * Proposition 5.1 and Theorem 5.2: with minimum degree ≥ 2, order five is the smallest order with a nonmonotone coordinate, for every ε ∈ (0, 1). The house covers ε > ε_c and the gem covers ε < ε_g ≈ 0.3565988.
  * Proposition 5.3 and Corollary 5.4: with leaves allowed, P3, K2 and K3 are μ-invariant. The four-vertex paw is nonmonotone iff ε ∈ (0, ε_1) ∪ (ε_2, 1), with ε_1 ≈ 0.0783255 and ε_2 ≈ 0.4355202. Every other four-vertex graph is monotone for every ε. So the smallest order with leaves allowed is 4 for ε < ε_1 or ε > ε_2, and 5 for ε_1 ≤ ε ≤ ε_2. Four vertices do not suffice at every damping value.
  * Lemma 5.6 and Corollary 5.7: failures lift through graph covers to every order 5k, with a fixed positive fraction of vertices nonmonotone.
  * Theorem 6.1, Proposition 6.2 and Corollary 6.3: a small-damping two-step criterion, an exact formula for dπ/dμ at μ = 1, and three cheap one-sided endpoint tests.
  * Theorem 9.1: the full score vector is invariant under backtracking iff P²v = v. Equality of the full vectors at μ = 1 and μ = ∞ at a single damping value is already equivalent.
  * Proposition 11.1 and Theorem 11.2: a one-sided bound nπ_∞(i) ≤ (1 + εd_i/2)/(1 + ε). The degree-tail Conjecture 3.2 fails for every tail index α > 1, in its local-weak-convergence reading; the clique-chain mechanism is adapted from Henning, van der Hofstad and Litvak.
* **Certified by exact computation, for the stated finite cases only.**
  * Proposition 5.5: a five-vertex tree (edges 04, 13, 23, 34) at ε = 1/2 has a nonmonotone leaf, with an exact certificate inside (0, 1). So trees are not always monotone.
  * Section 7, Table 3: a census of all 995 connected graphs on 2–7 vertices at ε ∈ {1/10, 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 17/20}, deciding monotonicity of every coordinate on [0, ∞) and on [0, 1]. Arithmetic is exact rational interpolation, squarefree decomposition and exact Sturm root counts on open intervals (roots at 0 and 1 divided out first); no floating point is used. For example, 353 of 507 seven-vertex graphs of minimum degree 2 have a nonmonotone coordinate for some listed ε.
  * Propositions 8.1 and 8.2: exact certificates that both readings of the spread clause fail. The reversed reading fails even on a seven-vertex graph whose maximiser and minimiser are unique for every μ.
  * Proposition 10.1: an exact transient rank reversal on the Les Misérables 2-core.
* **Numerical only.** Section 10, Table 4: prevalence on standard and random networks (10–41% of vertices nonmonotone), excursion sizes, frequency of shortcut failure, rank stability. Grid searches done in exact arithmetic at the grid points, such as the reversed-spread and rank-reversal searches in Sections 8 and 10, are evidence at those points only. They do not certify absence between grid points.
* **Open.** A structural classification of monotone coordinates; trees are not such a class (Proposition 5.5). A graph-level proof of the configuration-model tail transfer (Section 11). A structural condition under which the reversed spread clause holds; uniqueness of the extreme vertices is not enough (Proposition 8.2). A certified census at order 8 and over the whole damping interval. The effect of the node2vec in–out parameter.

Not covered: directed or weighted graphs, nonuniform restart (except Theorem 9.1 and Lemma 5.6), damping values between the five census values (the census only), and μ = 0 on graphs with leaves (where the source kernel is undefined; Section 2 explains the convention). No proof assistant was used. The journal version of the source was not accessible (HTTP 403), so the conjecture wording was checked against arXiv v2 only. The novelty statements rest on the bounded searches recorded in [RESEARCH_GATES.json](RESEARCH_GATES.json); they do not establish historical priority.
