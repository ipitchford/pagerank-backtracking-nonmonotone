# Review summary (internal, AI-assisted)

This is a public summary of the checks applied before release. It keeps the substantive objections and records how each was resolved. It is **not** peer review. Every reviewer was an AI system working inside the Evidence Press workflow. None of this is independent reproduction by an unaffiliated party, and none of it is formal verification. The original reports are kept privately. This summary contains no scores, ratings or recommendations.

## What was done (22 October 2026)

1. **First round.** An earlier 11-page draft (dated 20 October 2026) received a multi-perspective AI-assisted review. It had five parts: an editor's report; three specialist perspectives (methodology and exact computation; spectral graph theory and network centrality; applied network analysis); and a devil's-advocate challenge to the core argument. The reviewers re-solved the house graph in exact arithmetic, and reproduced the cubic roof formula, ε_c ≈ 0.24068, ε_0 ≈ 0.32910 and the minimum at μ = 1 for ε = 4/9. No reviewer found a mathematical error in a stated result. Their main criticisms concerned the framing of the conjecture, the scope of "smallest" and "complete", and missing extensions (below).
2. **Adversarial audit by a different model.** An AI-assisted adversarial audit by OpenAI gpt-6-astra was instructed to refute, not confirm. It rebuilt the full 12-arc house system symbolically, without the six-orbit quotient, and reproduced the roof formula identically. It also checked the classification's sign logic, enumerated all labelled graphs on at most four vertices, and ran 30 exact cover cases, 84 invariance cases, 48 endpoint/residual cases and six clique chains. Its scripts did not import package code. It found no critical or major defect, and one minor proof defect (M1, below).
3. **Revision.** The manuscript grew from 11 to 15 pages (17 in the final version). It was retitled and gained: the gem and paw propositions; minimality for every damping value; the certified census; the two-step criterion, slope formula and endpoint tests; both readings of the spread clause; the prevalence study; the exact rank reversal; the one-sided degree bound; and the degree-tail theorem for every tail index. The literature grew from 8 to 20 references (22 in the final version, which adds the Stanford GraphBase and NetworkX as data sources). The verification code was rebuilt as `code/verify_all.py`.
4. **Verification re-review.** The revised version was traced item by item against the fix list. The re-review recomputed certificates with its own exact solver (python-flint `fmpq`, no package code):
   * the house values at ε = 1/3, 1/2 and 17/20;
   * all eight house spread values and the reversed-spread witness;
   * the gem and paw witnesses;
   * the Les Misérables reversal.
   Every value agreed. It found no mathematical error, checked every table cell against the results files, and listed three textual corrections and some hygiene edits (below).
5. **Clean replay.** A separate copy of the revised package reproduced all three results files byte for byte.
6. **Second adversarial audit, of the revised version.** The same external model was run with three independent review workers. It found:
   * one major implementation error in the census root counter;
   * one medium edge case in the same routine;
   * one scope error in the conclusion;
   * two new exact counterexamples that answered questions the paper had listed as open (below).
   It confirmed the main counterexample, minimality under minimum degree two, the spread certificates, the two-step and slope tests, the repaired degree-tail construction and the Les Misérables reversal.
7. **Final fix round.** The root counter was replaced by exact Sturm counts on open intervals and the census regenerated. The census then agreed with the audit's independent Sturm implementation on all 33,900 coordinates, with no discrepancies. Corollary 5.4, Proposition 5.5 and Proposition 8.2 were added, and all re-review corrections were made. The final package was replayed byte-identically three times.

## Substantive objections and their resolution

| Objection | Resolution in this version |
|---|---|
| The source is published (Appl. Math. Comput. 531 (2026) 130182), but only arXiv was cited, and the conjectures were paraphrased. | Both versions are cited. Conjectures 3.1 and 3.2 are quoted verbatim from arXiv v2 (§1.1). The journal text was inaccessible (HTTP 403), and the paper states that its wording is unverified. |
| Conjecture 3.1 is a conjunction. Its spread clause (max − min nondecreasing in μ) was not addressed, and fails on the house, on the diamond and in random graphs. The printed inequality may be a reversal. | §1.1 separates the coordinatewise and spread clauses. Proposition 8.1 shows the printed clause fails on the diamond for every ε, and on the house at ε = 1/2 (eight exact values, strictly decreasing). The reversed clause also fails, on an exact six-vertex certificate at ε = 17/20. The wider search for reversed-clause failures is labelled as a grid search. |
| "Smallest counterexample" holds only under minimum degree ≥ 2, which the source does not assume. | Retitled. Theorem 5.2 keeps minimum degree ≥ 2 as an explicit hypothesis. Proposition 5.3 treats leaves: P3, K2 and K3 are invariant, and the paw fails iff ε ∈ (0, ε_1) ∪ (ε_2, 1). Corollary 5.4 gives the exact smallest order with leaves: 4 outside [ε_1, ε_2] and 5 on it. |
| "Complete classification" covers one vertex of one graph. | §4 is now titled "The complete damping classification of the roof vertex". |
| (Devil's advocate) A second order-5 graph of minimum degree two, the gem, fails at small damping. A grid search at larger damping saw only the house. | Resolved exactly. The census shows that the order-5 failures at the five census values are exactly the gem (ε ≤ 1/3) and the house (ε ≥ 1/4). Proposition 5.1 proves the gem fails for all ε < ε_g ≈ 0.3566. Together with the house, Theorem 5.2 makes order five minimal for every ε. |
| Minimality had no computational cross-check. | The certified census (§7) covers all connected graphs on 2–7 vertices, with and without the degree condition, and agrees with Theorem 5.2 and Proposition 5.3 at the census values. |
| No structural explanation of which vertices dip, and no practical test. | Theorem 6.1 gives a small-damping two-step criterion with the sign test σ_j. Proposition 6.2 gives an exact slope at μ = 1, and Corollary 6.3 three one-sided endpoint tests. The census checks them: the slope formula matched at 3985 vertices; the tests found 285 of 478 nonmonotone vertices at ε = 1/2 with no false positives. A heuristic explains why the roof needs ε > ε_c. |
| How common and how large are failures? Does any ranking actually change? | §10 gives a numerical prevalence table (10–41% of vertices; small excursions; shortcut wrong for up to 26%; Kendall τ ≥ 0.93). Proposition 10.1 is an exact transient rank reversal on the Les Misérables 2-core. |
| The endpoint residual bound certified nothing in practice. | Demoted to Remark 9.2. It states that the bound certified no pair on any test network, gives a coordinatewise version, and says that it implies nothing about μ = 0. |
| (Devil's advocate) The novelty of "equality at one damping value" in the invariance theorem is overstated. The arbitrary-restart converse is the real contribution. | Table 1 credits sufficiency to the source and to Arrigo et al., and lists only the converse as new. The audit confirmed that full-vector equality (not scalar equality) is what the theorem needs. |
| The degree-tail section covered one exponent, and gave no positive result. | Theorem 11.2 now covers every tail index α > 1. Proposition 11.1 gives the one-sided bound. The configuration-model positive transfer is stated as an open problem, with a heuristic only. The mechanism is credited to Henning, van der Hofstad and Litvak. |
| (Adversarial audit, M1) In the proof of the degree-tail theorem, "discard cliques incident to an endpoint or a size change" was under-specified. On the natural reading, the generic-score claim fails: for L = 4 the portal score is 43/36 instead of 42/36 at ε = 1/2. | The discarded set is now explicit: E_L = {1, 2, K−1, K} ∪ ⋃_{size changes}{j, j+1}. It removes O(L²) = o(n_L) vertices. The 43/36 example is in the proof. The verifier checks the generic scores on every surviving clique for β = 4 (L = 4, 5, 8, 12, 16) and β = 5 (L = 4, 6, 9), plus the 43/36 regression. The theorem's statement and limit law are unchanged. |
| (Adversarial audit) Scalar equality π_1(i) = π_∞(i) at one vertex does not imply invariance. A house example with nonuniform restart shows this. | Theorem 9.1 is stated for full vectors, which the audit confirmed is correct. |
| How are leaves handled? The source kernel is undefined at μ = 0 on a leaf. | §2 has a "Leaves" paragraph. It uses forced return (the continuous extension, which is also the node2vec convention), contrasts this with Aleja et al., and restricts every leaf-graph claim to μ > 0. Remark 2.1 adds the head/tail identity π = (1 − ε)v + εH, so monotonicity does not depend on the projection. |
| Literature too thin. The fibration basis of the cover lemma, second-order PageRank, node2vec, local weak convergence and non-backtracking spectral work were missing. | 12 references added, all DOIs checked via Crossref ([CITATION_AUDIT.md](CITATION_AUDIT.md)). Lemma 5.6 is credited as an instance of fibration invariance (Boldi et al.). |
| Hard to read: no plain-language summary, roadmap, intuition or results table. | Added a plain-language abstract, a roadmap, Table 1 (status and novelty of every result), an intuition section, a coefficient table, small-graph drawings and a conclusions/open-problems section. |
| (Re-review) The conclusion said the smallest failures have "five vertices (four with leaves) at every damping value". That is false for ε ∈ [ε_1, ε_2], where the paw is monotone and the census finds no order-4 failure. | Corrected in §13, and made exact by Corollary 5.4. With minimum degree two the smallest order is five for every ε. With leaves it is four for ε < ε_1 or ε > ε_2 and five otherwise. The second audit raised the same point independently, proving monotonicity of P4 symbolically and invariance of the star. |
| (Re-review) Remark 2.1 called the model the unweighted special case of Aleja et al., but dropped "leaf-free". That contradicts the leaf paragraph. | "Leaf-free" restored. The leaf paragraph now also says that the source kernel is undefined at a leaf when μ = 0, and that the forced return there is the paper's own continuity choice. |
| (Re-review) Two grid searches (reversed spread clause; absence of rank reversals on seven-vertex graphs) were worded as if they were exact certificates of absence. | Both are now labelled as discovery or finite searches: exact arithmetic at the grid points, no certificate between them. Only the displayed witnesses are certified. |
| (Re-review) Smaller points: the gem-cover sentence in the cover corollary did not carry over; excursion medians were misquoted; references to the drafting process remained in the text and code comments. | Corollary 5.7 now states the gem-cover family separately: degrees two, three and four, with at least two fifths of the vertices nonmonotone for ε < ε_g. The median range is corrected (0.004% to 1.5%). The process references are removed. |
| (Second audit, major) The census root-isolation routine could record a root in (0, 1) as a reversed interval, and then miss it. 27 coordinates were misclassified on (0, 1), so ten order-7 table entries were wrong. The minimal regression is N = 4μ³ − 15μ² + 12μ. Atlas graph 399 at ε = 1/2 is a real witness. | Root counting was replaced by exact Sturm counts on open intervals, with endpoint roots divided out first. Regressions now cover both synthetic responses, Atlas 399, and every subset of a known root set (256 cases). The census was regenerated. Corrected order-7 counts on (0, 1), at ε = 1/10, 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 17/20: 50/68/75/85/44 for minimum degree two, and 113/141/156/172/89 for all connected graphs. All counts on (0, ∞), the some/all counts and the headlines 353/507 and 675/853 were unchanged. The final census agrees with the audit's independent Sturm counter on all 33,900 coordinates. |
| (Second audit, medium) A root at μ = 0 could hide a root in (0, 1) through the endpoint sign probe (example N = 4μ³ − 3μ², D = 1). It did not occur in the census, but the helper was wrong in general. | Fixed by the same replacement. A root at 0 counts for neither range; a root at 1 counts for (0, ∞) but not for (0, 1). Both synthetic cases are regression tests. |
| (Second audit) Open problem 1 asked whether every coordinate of a tree is monotone. | Answered negatively by an exact counterexample, now Proposition 5.5. In the five-vertex tree with edges 04, 13, 23, 34 at ε = 1/2, leaf 1 dips to a strict minimum at μ ≈ 0.2645, with a certificate inside (0, 1). The open problem is narrowed accordingly. |

## Remaining limitations

* The census is certified at five damping values only, and only up to order seven. Its correctness on (0, 1) rests on the repaired Sturm counter and the regressions above; the counts on (0, ∞) were unaffected by the earlier error.
* The configuration-model tail transfer is unproved.
* The journal wording of the conjectures is unverified because of a paywall; arXiv v2 was checked verbatim.
* The prevalence and ranking statistics are numerical, apart from one exact reversal.
* No structural classification of monotone coordinates is known. Trees are not such a class, and unique extrema do not rescue the reversed spread clause.
* No proof assistant was used. No unaffiliated party has reproduced the results. The novelty search was bounded. The source authors have not been contacted about this work.
